02 — HOW I THINK
Principles, not just software

How I think and work as an estimator

A proactive, structured workflow: I take ownership of the next action, keep the work traceable and raise questions before uncertainty becomes a costly assumption.

  • Start with the brief. Set up the tender file, understand deadlines, contract conditions, scope boundaries and required deliverables.
  • Read across the documents. Review drawings, specifications, schedules and reports together; identify gaps, conflicts, revisions and missing information.
  • Package the scope. Break the project into logical trades and work sections so inclusions, exclusions and interfaces are visible.
  • Clarify early. Prepare RFIs, record assumptions and track responses rather than silently guessing when information is incomplete.
  • Measure systematically. Complete trade-by-trade take-offs using consistent units, documented dimensions, deductions, allowances and traceable backup.
  • Build and review the price. Separate direct costs, preliminaries, overheads, risk, escalation and margin; check quantities, rates, formulas and scope coverage. I work efficiently while protecting the time needed for verification.
  • Take initiative and keep work moving. I review the task, identify the next required action and begin work without waiting for every step to be assigned. I ask focused questions when information or authority is needed, keep the client informed of blockers and take responsibility for agreed deliverables.
  • Use technology responsibly. AI can support speed and cross-checking, but I verify outputs against source documents and professional judgment before anything reaches a client.

My working method is structured around the 13-step tender sequence in my Australian Construction Estimating Handbook: receive and set up the tender; review contract conditions; examine drawings, specifications and reports; define scope and trade packages; raise and track RFIs; request and compare quotations; complete quantity take-offs; price the work; allow for preliminaries and site establishment; assess overheads, risk, escalation and margin; carry out review and sanity checks; prepare the submission; and manage post-tender follow-up. I am applying this sequence in Australian-market study and practice exercises. My priority is to complete work as efficiently as possible without sacrificing accuracy, traceability or timely communication.
